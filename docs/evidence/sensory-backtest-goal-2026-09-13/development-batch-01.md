Use 5,742 eligible 2019–2021 training games and 2,429 eligible 2022 development games from the frozen development source. Build the existing 48-hour delayed, UTC-day-batched pregame features. No 2023 data access is permitted in this batch. Exclusions and score validation are fixed in `data-eligibility.md`.

Fit three L2 logistic encoders, C={0.01,0.1,1}, on training-only standardized home-minus-away vectors: Elo, five-game form, cumulative win rate, cumulative mean margin and capped rest. A fitted intercept represents venue. Each team gets its own linear strength score relative to an average training team; add half the venue coefficient to the home opportunity and subtract half for the away opportunity. Apply the same score-to-quality function to both. Center and scale the distribution of individual training-team scores, then apply a sigmoid. This is an absolute training-reference quality scale, not a win probability and not pairwise min/max scaling. The encoder-only matchup probability is the original logistic prediction. Name venue as an engineered pregame covariate.

For each encoder, use 100 complete-ISO-week training bootstrap resamples (seed20260913) to estimate parameter uncertainty in individual quality; retain the 2.5th/97.5th percentiles widened to include the point estimate. Until 50 prior games are available, use [0,1] instead. These are model-parameter intervals with an explicit cold-start guard, not calibrated future-performance intervals. The fixed training quality transform is reused for bootstrap coefficients. Both teams may therefore receive sweet patterns; every side uses the identical rule.

This is exact reuse of responses to identical fixed schedules, not a learned neural surrogate. No neural plasticity, feature-to-ALPN bypass, direct pregame feature at the readout, active-model update or 2024+ input. Probabilities come from this fitted external readout, not an innate choice mechanism.
