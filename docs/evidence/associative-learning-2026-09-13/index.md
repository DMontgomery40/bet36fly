## Sources checked for this stage

- MaleCNS v1.0 files pinned in `docs/connectome-source-lock.json` (annotations, neurotransmitters, weights; release 2026-06-08, checked locally 2026-09-13). Cell types used: PAM08 (γ4), PAM01 (γ5), MBON05 (γ4>γ1γ2), MBON01 (γ5β′2a), KCg-*, ORN_* (53 types), ALLN, APL, LB3b/c sweet cells from the Tastekin 2026 crosswalk already recorded in the sensory evidence.
- Shiu et al. 2024 reference simulator `model.py` at revision 91bdd1e7dcf193f3e7ca5a8933497fcef63b7960 (LIF constants, 68.75 mV events, sensory refractory exemption), inspected earlier in this repository and reused unchanged.
- Primary sources rechecked on 2026-09-14 UTC through the Europe PMC REST service and eLife (PubMed itself refused the fetch without cookies); the repository wiki records none of the reward-DAN or MBON-valence assignments, which therefore rest on these records only:
  - Liu et al. 2012, Nature, DOI 10.1038/nature11304, PMID 22810589: a PAM-cluster dopamine subset signals sugar reward by transient activation and projects to the medial lobes where appetitive memory forms.
  - Huetteroth et al. 2015, Current Biology, DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2015.01.036, PMID 25728694: OAMB-expressing dopamine neurons projecting to **β′2 and γ4** convey the short-term reinforcing effect of sweet taste; nutrient-dependent long-term memory uses neurons projecting to **γ5b** (and, with artificial reinforcement, β lobe and α1). Consequence for this stage: γ4 (PAM08) is the compartment most directly matched to sweet-taste reward; γ5 (PAM01) is a sugar-reward compartment for nutrient/long-term memory rather than taste per se. Both remain in the protocol as declared; this distinction is now recorded.
  - Yamagata et al. 2015, PNAS, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1421930112, PMID 25548178: two distinct PAM subsets signal reward for short-term versus long-term memory, targeting spatially segregated mushroom-body domains (compartment names are in the full text, not the abstract).
  - Aso et al. 2014, eLife 04580 (companion to 04577): optogenetic activation of glutamatergic MBONs, including MBON-γ5β′2a, MBON-β′2mp and MBON-γ4>γ1γ2, elicits avoidance; GABAergic and cholinergic MBONs elicit attraction. This is the basis for reading appetitive memory as reduced odor drive of MBON05/MBON01.
  - Hige et al. 2015, Neuron, DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2015.11.003, PMID 26637800: pairing odor with activation of specific dopamine neurons induces odor-specific synaptic depression at the MBON with temporal specificity; the overlap of sparse KC representations sets both specificity and generalization (the generalization we measured between cues A/B is the same phenomenon).
  - Handler et al. 2019, Cell, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2019.05.040, PMID 31230716: the relative timing of odor and dopamine determines depression versus potentiation through DopR1 and DopR2 coupled to distinct second messengers. The wiki adds that the reporter preparations concern γ4/γ5 and that Hige 2015 did not find a universal opposite-sign backward effect; our backward arm's potentiation is therefore a prediction of the declared rule confirmed in the simulator, not a universal fly result.
- Jiang & Litwin-Kumar 2021 (PLOS CB, `runmodel.py` at a16f86a3) for the opposing-term rule shape; Hige 2015, Cohn 2015, Handler 2019 for KC-then-dopamine depression / dopamine-then-KC potentiation; Aso 2014 and Yamagata 2015 for PAM-γ4/γ5 sugar reward and MBON05/MBON01 appetitive memory expression; Zhao 2022 for the 118 Hz odor anchor. These were read from the repository wiki's source ledger on 2026-09-13; no new external retrieval was needed for cell identities, which come from the pinned dataset.
